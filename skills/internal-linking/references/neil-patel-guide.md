# Neil Patel's guide to internal links: summary

Source: [O Guia do Neil Patel Para Links Internos](https://neilpatel.com/br/blog/o-guia-do-neil-patel-para-links-internos/) (English version: [The Complete Guide to Internal Linking](https://neilpatel.com/blog/the-complete-guide-to-internal-linking/)). This is a paraphrased summary of the parts the skill relies on.

## What internal links are

A link from one page of a site to another page of the same site. Neil calls most discussions of site architecture overcomplicated. Links between articles are what produce results.

## Why they matter

- **Indexing:** links help Google's crawler find new content, so it's crawled and indexed faster.
- **Deep pages:** most backlinks from other sites point to the homepage. Strong internal linking raises crawl priority across the whole site and makes deeper pages more likely to earn links.
- **Link value:** a well-structured set of internal links passes value from established pages to others ("link juice" is a simplification, but the effect is real).
- **Anchor text:** a text link with descriptive anchor text adds value, as long as it isn't over-optimized with keywords.
- **Readers (the most important reason):** links give readers more on the topic. Do it for them even if you ignore the SEO benefits.
- **Funnel:** links don't convert directly, but they move readers toward the pages that do.

## Techniques

Prerequisites: some existing content (one article is enough to start) and new content published regularly.

1. **Link content-rich pages to content-rich pages.** Article to article, preferably long-form. Don't link to the homepage, About or Contact pages, since the navigation covers them. The exception is a sales or squeeze page when it fits.
2. **Use descriptive anchor text** that says what the target page is about, inside a sentence about that subject. Avoid:
   - exact-match keyword anchors, associated with Penguin penalties,
   - "click here" ("clique aqui"), which adds nothing,
   - links spanning more than one sentence. A few words or one sentence is the right size.
3. **When writing a new article, link to 4 or more older articles** (the body text of the guide says 5 or more; the skill uses 5 as the target and 4 as the minimum). New links on an established site add freshness value.
4. **Update old articles with links to new ones.** Combine it with refreshing the content: a new intro paragraph explaining the update, new or updated paragraphs throughout, and links to recent articles. An established page then links to a newer, less established one.
5. **Put links where they're logical and valuable,** in the body where the topics overlap. Never in a generic block at the end.
6. **Keep links followed.** Don't add `nofollow` to internal links. It's unnecessary and probably passes less value.
7. **Link to high-converting pages.** Find the pages that convert visitors best and link to them from related content. More visitors there means more conversions.

## Conclusion

Internal linking is simple and effective. Neil suggests following the guidance for at least a week and checking whether rankings improve.
